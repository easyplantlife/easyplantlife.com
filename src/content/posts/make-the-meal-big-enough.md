You finish dinner and feel fine.

An hour later you are in the kitchen again. The cupboard is open. You are looking for something that will actually stay with you.

The meal was pleasant. It was also small.

A handful of leaves. A few roasted vegetables. A plate that looked finished and was not.

It is easy, in that moment, to decide the whole way of eating is the problem.

You need a new plan. You need different food. You need to try again tomorrow.

Often the dinner in front of you was simply too small, and you can still add to it.

## A Light Plate Is Still a Small Meal

There is a kind of plate that gets treated as the correct one.

Most of it is empty. The food is arranged with care. The portion is modest, as if a modest plate and a good meal were the same thing.

It looks tidy.

It does not carry you to the next time you eat.

If you are hungry again before the evening is over, the plate did not do its job. That is not a verdict on you. It is a description of the portion.

Leaves belong at dinner. Roasted vegetables belong at dinner. A spoonful of grains belongs at dinner. In small amounts, with nothing else, they are a start that never became a meal.

![A wide white plate with only a small handful of salad leaves and a fork, most of the plate empty.](/images/blog/make-the-meal-big-enough/a-small-plate-of-leaves.jpg "Leaves can be part of dinner. They are rarely all of it.")

## Add the Food That Stays

A meal that lasts usually has something plain in it, and enough of that thing to notice.

Rice. Potatoes. Bread. Noodles. Beans. Lentils. Tofu. A thick soup.

Not all of them. One or two is plenty.

This is not a new standard. It is what was true about the last dinner that worked. The bowl was deep. There was bread on the table. You did not go looking an hour later.

Keep the vegetables if you like them. Put them next to something that takes up space.

Broccoli with a full bowl of rice.

A salad with beans, and bread beside it.

Lentils with potatoes, not a spoon of lentils on an otherwise empty plate.

The vegetables are still there. They are no longer being asked to be the entire meal.

## The Pot Can Stay on the Stove

A first plate is not a limit.

If you are still hungry, eat more of what you made. The second bowl is part of dinner. It is not a correction of the first one.

Leave the pot out until you know you are done.

Some evenings you eat once and stop. Some evenings you come back a few minutes later. Both are ordinary.

What helps is making a little more than the careful amount you first pictured. Another ladle. A second handful of noodles. Bread already cut, sitting on the board.

You are not preparing the week. You are letting this meal be large enough to finish the job.

![A pot of lentil stew still on the stove with a wooden ladle, and a filled bowl beside it.](/images/blog/make-the-meal-big-enough/the-pot-still-on-the-stove.jpg "The pot can stay out until you are done.")

## Hunger Is Information About That Dinner

When a small meal disappears quickly, the explanation tends to get large.

The food must have been missing something. The evening must need a system. Tomorrow must be different.

Usually the missing thing was quantity.

A full bowl of rice and beans will keep you in a way that a taste of rice and beans will not. The ingredients did not change. The amount did.

If a particular food never satisfies you, no matter how much of it you eat, choose a different one. You do not have to keep a meal that leaves you hunting through the cupboard. Eat the ones that last.

Hunger after dinner is telling you something about that dinner.

It is not asking you to rebuild the way you eat.

## Enough Looks Ordinary

A meal that is big enough rarely looks delicate.

The bowl is filled higher than a styled plate. The bread is a real slice. The soup comes up the side of the bowl. There is enough that someone else could have some and you would still be fed.

If you are used to plates that perform restraint, this can look like too much.

It is just dinner.

You do not need to announce the portion. You do not need to earn it with a smaller lunch. You do not need to balance it later by eating less on purpose.

Eat until the meal has done what a meal is for.

Stop because you are fed.

![A deep bowl of lentil stew, two slices of bread, and roasted potatoes on a wooden table.](/images/blog/make-the-meal-big-enough/bread-potatoes-and-stew.jpg "Enough is a full bowl, bread, and something that stays.")

## Start With More of the Same Food

The next time dinner disappears too quickly, do not begin with a new idea.

Begin with more of the food you already have.

Use a deeper bowl. Put bread on the table. Leave the pot where you can reach it.

If you are hungry later, that can mean you needed more. You can still go and eat.

A plant-based meal does not have to be light to be simple.

It has to be enough.
