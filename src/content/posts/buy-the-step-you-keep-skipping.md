You know what you would eat.

You also know the step you are not going to do.

The onion that needs chopping. The sauce that starts from a clove of garlic. The greens that need washing. The grain that needs most of an hour.

So the meal does not start.

The food was never the difficult part. One step was. And that step is often something you can buy already done.

## Name the Step

Before you decide you are not cooking, name the part you are avoiding.

Sometimes it is the knife. Sometimes it is the pot. Sometimes it is a sauce you do not feel like building from nothing.

The rest of the meal may already be easy. There is bread. There are noodles. There is a can of beans. There is something in the freezer.

None of it gets used, because the missing step is sitting in front of all of it.

Once you can see which step it is, dinner stops being a large problem. It becomes one small job, and that job might already be finished.

## Finished Parts Are Still Ingredients

A tub of hummus is an ingredient.

So is a jar of tomato sauce. A box of leaves that are already washed. A loaf of bread. A can of beans. A packet of grains someone else already cooked, if that is what you will actually eat.

These are not a lesser version of dinner.

They are the first step, moved out of your evening.

Opening a jar is not a failure to cook. It is a way of removing the part that was going to stop you, and keeping the part you are willing to do.

![An open jar of tomato sauce, a plain silver can, and a cardboard box of washed greens on a wooden counter.](/images/blog/buy-the-step-you-keep-skipping/parts-already-finished.jpg "Someone else did the first step.")

## Keep the Rest Small

Once one part arrives finished, it is easy to complicate the meal again.

A second sauce. A garnish. A vegetable that still needs a cutting board. A method you saw once and have not tried.

That hands the evening its obstacle back.

When one part is already done, let the meal stay at two or three things.

Bread, hummus, and cucumber.

Noodles, a jar of sauce, and something from the freezer.

Washed greens, a can of beans, and toast.

Warm what needs warming. Salt it. Put it on a plate.

Then leave it there.

![Two slices of bread spread with hummus and cucumber on a plain plate, with a few extra cucumber slices beside them.](/images/blog/buy-the-step-you-keep-skipping/bread-hummus-and-cucumber.jpg "Bread, hummus, cucumber. Dinner.")

## Buy the Version You Finish

A finished ingredient only helps if you open it.

The interesting jar, the one that promises a certain kind of evening, can sit in the cupboard the same way unused herbs sit in the fridge. It asked for a version of the night you did not have.

Buy the plain version you already know you like.

The hummus you finish. The sauce you put on noodles without a plan. The bread you toast. The greens you have eaten before.

Keep one of each, where you can see them.

When one is gone, replace that one. A shelf of options is another decision. The step you usually skip can be the one that is already there.

![A wooden shelf with one tub of hummus, one jar of sauce, and a loaf of bread, with empty space around them.](/images/blog/buy-the-step-you-keep-skipping/one-of-each-on-the-shelf.jpg "One of each. Enough room to see them.")

## Make It When You Want To

None of this is a rule against starting from the beginning.

Some evenings you want the knife. You want the onion. You want the sauce to be yours. Those are good evenings to do the step yourself.

The bought version is there for the other evenings.

The ones where dinner is about to become something you postpone until you are no longer hungry enough to care.

You can still cook the parts you enjoy.

You do not have to cook the part that keeps you out of the kitchen.

## Let That Step Arrive Done

The next time a meal stalls, do not look for a new plan.

Look for the step.

If it is the sauce, buy the sauce. If it is the washing and the cutting, buy the vegetable that is already prepared. If it is the bread, buy the loaf.

Then make the small meal around it.

You do not need every part of dinner to come from your hands.

You need dinner to happen.

The step you keep skipping can arrive done.

The rest can be ordinary. That is enough.
