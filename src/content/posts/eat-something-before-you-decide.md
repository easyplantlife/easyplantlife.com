You open the fridge because you are hungry.

Nothing looks like the right answer. The leftovers would need a plan. The vegetables would need a pan. The thing you meant to cook would need a version of the evening you do not have.

So you close the door.

You open a cupboard. You close that too.

You are still standing in the kitchen, and you are hungrier than you were a minute ago.

This is a poor moment to decide dinner.

## The Choice Gets Worse When You Are Empty

Hunger makes two feelings show up together.

Simple food starts to look like it will not be enough.

Anything that takes more than a few minutes starts to look impossible.

You want a real meal, and you do not want to begin one. Both can be true at the same time. The fridge does not resolve it. Standing there longer does not either.

What you are trying to do is choose, search your own kitchen, and start cooking, all while the only thing your body wants is to eat.

That is a lot to ask of an open door.

![An open refrigerator with broccoli, two apples, and a jar on mostly empty shelves.](/images/blog/eat-something-before-you-decide/the-open-fridge.jpg "Everything in here looks like a decision.")

## Eat the Thing That Needs No Plan

Before you decide what dinner is, eat something that is already finished.

A piece of fruit. A slice of bread. A few crackers, if that is what you keep. Whatever is in the kitchen and does not require a pot.

Not a snack plan. Not a rule about what should come first.

Just enough that you are no longer choosing from empty.

Sit down with it if you can. The standing is part of the loop. A chair interrupts it.

Then look at the kitchen again.

The meal that felt like too much often looks ordinary once you are slightly less hungry. Toast and something from a jar. Noodles. A pot of lentils. The leftovers you were about to ignore.

You did not become more organized in ten minutes.

You became someone who can see the food.

![An apple with one bite taken and a slice of bread on a small plate.](/images/blog/eat-something-before-you-decide/bread-and-an-apple.jpg "Enough to stop deciding from empty.")

## The First Bite Is Not the Whole Meal

There is a small worry attached to this.

If you eat something first, you might spoil dinner. Or you might keep eating at the counter and never make a plate.

Sometimes the bread and the apple are the meal, and you are done. If there is enough of it, that can be dinner. There is no need to start a second project to prove you cooked.

More often the first bite is small on purpose. It is there to break the pause. It is not there to replace the plate.

Finish it. Notice whether you are still hungry.

If you are, cook the ordinary thing.

If you are not, stop.

Both are allowed. The point is that you chose after you had eaten something, not before.

## Keep One Thing for This Moment

The food that helps here is the food you do not have to think about.

An apple in a bowl you walk past. A loaf you are willing to cut. Bread that is already sliced. Something plain you have eaten on a tired evening before.

It does not need to be interesting. It needs to be there on the day you come home empty.

If every edible thing in the kitchen requires a plan, this moment has nothing to use. That is worth noticing the next time you shop.

You do not need a new category of groceries.

You need one thing you can eat as it is.

Put it where you will see it. A bowl by the door. The end of the counter. Somewhere the decision is already half made, because the food is in front of you.

![A wooden bowl of apples and a banana on the counter beside a kitchen doorway.](/images/blog/eat-something-before-you-decide/fruit-by-the-door.jpg "One thing you can eat as it is.")

## Then Choose the Meal You Already Know

Once you have eaten a little, go back to the meal you already know how to make.

You do not need a better idea than the one you had yesterday. You need a little less urgency.

Put water on. Open the can. Toast the bread. Warm the lentils.

The decision is smaller now. It fits in the time you actually have.

If you are still hungry after the first bite, let the plate be a full one. Soup, bread, whatever you already trust. The first bite was a start. Dinner can still be dinner.

![A bowl of lentil soup with a slice of bread beside it on a wooden table.](/images/blog/eat-something-before-you-decide/lentils-and-bread.jpg "The ordinary meal, once you can see it.")

## You Can Stop Standing There

The long pause in the kitchen feels like a lack of follow-through.

It is usually hunger asking you to solve two problems at once.

Eat.

And decide.

Separate them.

Eat something small, the kind that needs no plan.

Then decide.

Most evenings, that is enough to get dinner started. Some evenings, the small thing was the meal, and you can let it be.

Either way, you are no longer standing in front of an open fridge, waiting to feel ready.
